\# BLINKITA METHOD™



\# DOCUMENT MATURITY MODEL



Version: 0.1



Status: Active Framework



\---



\# Purpose



The Document Maturity Model defines how BLINKITA METHOD™ documents evolve through different stages of development.



Its purpose is to make the state of knowledge visible.



A living methodology requires not only memory of ideas.



It requires memory of their maturity.



\---



\# Principle



Not every document represents the same level of certainty.



Some documents describe:



\- exploration;

\- emerging concepts;

\- established methodology;

\- validated knowledge.



The maturity status protects clarity between:



possibility



↓



development



↓



application



↓



validation



\---



\# Maturity Levels



Documents may have the following statuses.



\---



\# Draft



\## Meaning



Early working material.



A Draft document may contain:



\- incomplete ideas;

\- unfinished structures;

\- exploratory thinking.



\## Usage



Internal development.



\## Requirements



No external dependency should rely on a Draft document.



\---



\# Proposed



\## Meaning



A structured concept awaiting wider review.



A Proposed document has:



\- clear purpose;

\- defined scope;

\- initial structure.



\## Usage



Evaluation and discussion.



\---



\# Experimental



\## Meaning



A document or concept actively being tested.



Experimental documents may include:



\- assumptions;

\- alternative approaches;

\- unresolved questions.



\## Usage



Research and learning.



\---



\# Active



\## Meaning



A currently used methodology document.



An Active document has:



\- defined purpose;

\- stable structure;

\- known relationships.



\## Usage



Operational application.



\---



\# Canonical



\## Meaning



A protected reference document.



Canonical documents define:



\- identity;

\- essential principles;

\- controlled terminology;

\- foundational structures.



\## Requirements



Canonical documents require governance review before major changes.



\---



\# Validated



\## Meaning



A document or concept supported by documented evidence.



Validation may include:



\- internal observation;

\- external application;

\- research evidence.



Validation does not mean permanent truth.



It means current evidence supports continued use.



\---



\# Deprecated



\## Meaning



A previous document or concept that is no longer recommended.



Deprecated documents remain preserved for:



\- historical memory;

\- evolution tracking;

\- research.



\---



\# Status Transition



Documents evolve through:



Draft



↓



Proposed



↓



Experimental



↓



Active



↓



Canonical



↓



Validated





A document may also move to:





Deprecated





when replaced or discontinued.



\---



\# Required Document Metadata



Important documents should include:





Title



Version



Status



Purpose



Owner Layer



Related Documents



Last Review Date





\---



\# Relationship With Governance



Document maturity does not replace governance.



Governance defines:



how change happens.



Document maturity defines:



what state the document is in.



\---



\# Relationship With Research



Research may influence maturity changes.



Examples:



Observation



↓



Experimental



↓



Evidence



↓



Active



↓



Validated



\---



\# Relationship With Canon



Canonical status requires:



\- conceptual stability;

\- glossary alignment;

\- governance approval;

\- architectural consistency.



\---



\# Repository Application



Future repository development should gradually add maturity metadata to important documents.



Priority:



1\. Canon documents

2\. Frameworks

3\. Standards

4\. Blueprints

5\. Playbooks

6\. Research records



\---



\# Final Principle



A living methodology does not hide uncertainty.



It organizes uncertainty.



Maturity labels allow creation, research and evolution to exist together without confusion.
