\# BLINKITA METHOD™



\# VERSIONING



\## Evolution Model of the Methodology



\---



\# Purpose



This document defines how versions of the BLINKITA METHOD™ are understood and documented.



Versioning is not only a technical tracking system.



It represents the evolution, maturity and transformation of the methodology itself.



\---



\# Core Principle



BLINKITA METHOD™ is treated as a Living Methodology™.



Each version represents a new stage of understanding, organization, validation or expansion.



Versions preserve the journey from early concepts toward increasingly refined structures.



\---



\# Version Structure



BLINKITA METHOD™ follows:



\## Major Version



Represents a fundamental evolution of the methodology.



Examples:



\- new architectural layers

\- new foundational principles

\- significant paradigm shifts

\- expanded methodology scope





Format:

vX.0.0





\---



\## Minor Version



Represents meaningful development within the existing architecture.



Examples:



\- new frameworks

\- new blueprints

\- new playbooks

\- refined standards

\- expanded research documentation





Format:





v0.X.0





\---



\## Revision Version



Represents refinement and improvement.



Examples:



\- clarification

\- corrections

\- documentation improvements

\- terminology refinement





Format:





v0.0.X





\---



\# Development Stages



The evolution of BLINKITA METHOD™ may include:



\---



\## Stage 0 — Exploration



Ideas, observations and early concepts emerge.



\---



\## Stage 1 — Foundation



Core philosophy, principles and terminology are established.



\---



\## Stage 2 — Architecture



Frameworks, blueprints and structural relationships are created.



\---



\## Stage 3 — Application



Playbooks, standards and implementation processes are tested.



\---



\## Stage 4 — Validation



Case studies, research observations and external applications contribute evidence.



\---



\## Stage 5 — Evolution



The methodology expands through learning, adaptation and new discoveries.



\---



\# Version History Principle



Every version is part of the research record.



Previous versions should not be considered obsolete.



They represent the state of understanding at a specific moment in time.
