\# BLINKITA METHOD™



\# EVIDENCE HIERARCHY



Version: 0.1



Status: Active Research Framework



\---



\# Purpose



The Evidence Hierarchy defines how BLINKITA METHOD™ distinguishes between:



\- philosophical principles;

\- conceptual models;

\- assumptions;

\- hypotheses;

\- observations;

\- evidence;

\- validated methodology components.



Its purpose is to preserve intellectual honesty while allowing continuous exploration and evolution.



\---



\# Core Principle



A Living Method™ can contain vision, exploration and research.



However:



possibility



is not the same as



evidence.



The purpose of evidence hierarchy is not to reduce creativity.



It is to protect clarity.



\---



\# Evidence Levels



BLINKITA METHOD™ recognizes the following levels.



\---



\# Level 1 — Philosophical Principle



\## Definition



A foundational belief, perspective or worldview element that guides the methodology.



\## Examples



\- Creation can evolve beyond its initial form.

\- Memory supports continuity.

\- Structure enables meaningful creation.



\## Validation Requirement



Philosophical principles are not validated as scientific facts.



They are evaluated through:



\- coherence;

\- usefulness;

\- alignment with methodology.



\---



\# Level 2 — Conceptual Proposition



\## Definition



A developed idea describing a possible relationship between concepts.



\## Examples



\- Living Memory™ may support continuity.

\- Creator Navigation™ may reduce complexity.



\## Validation Requirement



Requires conceptual consistency and relationship mapping.



\---



\# Level 3 — Working Assumption



\## Definition



A temporary assumption used to guide development.



\## Examples



\- A unified creation map may improve orientation.

\- Structured reflection may support clearer decisions.



\## Validation Requirement



Requires further observation or research.



\---



\# Level 4 — Research Hypothesis



\## Definition



A testable proposition that defines:



\- what is expected;

\- who or what is observed;

\- what outcome may occur;

\- what evidence could challenge it.



\---



\# Required Hypothesis Elements



A research hypothesis should define:



\## Target



Who or what is being studied.



\---



\## Intervention



What is introduced or changed.



\---



\## Expected Outcome



What improvement or effect is expected.



\---



\## Measurement



How the outcome may be observed.



\---



\## Disconfirming Evidence



What result would challenge the hypothesis.



\---



\# Level 5 — Internal Observation



\## Definition



An observation collected through application within BLINKITA development.



Examples:



\- creator experience;

\- development process;

\- implementation learning.



\## Limitation



Internal observation does not automatically prove general applicability.



\---



\# Level 6 — External Observation



\## Definition



Observation from creators, users or environments outside the original development context.



\## Importance



External observation reduces founder-specific interpretation.



\---



\# Level 7 — Comparative Evidence



\## Definition



Evidence created through comparison with:



\- alternative methods;

\- different approaches;

\- existing systems.



\---



\# Level 8 — Validated Method Component



\## Definition



A methodology element supported by sufficient evidence for continued application.



Validation requires:



\- documented evidence;

\- clear boundaries;

\- known limitations;

\- repeatable application.



\---



\# Evidence Does Not Mean Permanence



Validation means:



"Current evidence supports continued use."



It does not mean:



"Future change is impossible."



A Living Method™ remains open to learning.

\# Claim Language Rules



Documents should use language appropriate to evidence level.



\## Philosophical



"BLINKITA METHOD™ recognizes..."



\## Conceptual



"The methodology proposes..."



\## Hypothesis



"This hypothesis explores whether..."



\## Observation



"Initial observations indicate..."



\## Validation



"Evidence currently supports..."
