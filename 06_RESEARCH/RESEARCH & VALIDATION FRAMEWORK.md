\# Purpose



The BLINKITA METHOD™ Research \& Validation Framework defines how the methodology learns, evolves and evaluates its own development.



The purpose of research is not to prove predetermined conclusions.



The purpose is to:



\- observe;

\- document;

\- learn;

\- improve;

\- refine;

\- understand.



\---



\# Research Position



BLINKITA METHOD™ is an evolving methodology.



Its concepts exist at different maturity levels.



Research helps distinguish between:



\- philosophical principles;

\- conceptual models;

\- working hypotheses;

\- observed patterns;

\- validated components.



\---

\# Claim Boundaries



Different levels require different language.



\## Philosophical Principle



Defines foundational beliefs and perspectives.



Example:



"Memory supports continuity."



\---



\## Conceptual Proposition



Defines a possible relationship between concepts.



Example:



"Living Memory™ may support long-term creative continuity."



\---



\## Research Hypothesis



Defines something that can be explored.



Example:



"Creators using structured memory systems may resume interrupted work more effectively."



\---



\## Observation



Documents what happened in a specific context.



Example:



"A creator reported improved clarity after organizing previous decisions."



\---



\## Validated Method Component



Requires stronger evidence:



\- repeated application;

\- independent users;

\- documented outcomes;

\- comparison where possible;

\- external review.



\---



\# What Validation Means



Within BLINKITA METHOD™, validation means:



A structured process of evaluating whether a methodological element demonstrates consistent usefulness within defined conditions.



Validation does not mean:



\- universal truth;

\- guaranteed success;

\- superiority over all alternatives.



\---



\# Validation Criteria



A methodological component becomes stronger when it demonstrates:



\## Clarity



Creators understand the process and intended purpose.



\---



\## Usability



Creators can apply the process.



\---



\## Reproducibility



Similar applications produce recognisable outputs.



\---



\## Meaningfulness



The process provides valuable insight or improvement.



\---



\## Adaptability



The process remains useful across different contexts.

\# Validation Levels



\## Level 0 — Conceptual



A concept exists and is documented.



Evidence:



\- definitions;

\- relationships;

\- theoretical structure.



\---



\## Level 1 — Internal Exploration



The concept is applied within BLINKITA.



Evidence:



\- development records;

\- observations;

\- reflections.



\---



\## Level 2 — Documented Application



The concept is applied in additional cases.



Evidence:



\- case studies;

\- outputs;

\- user feedback.



\---



\## Level 3 — External Observation



Independent creators apply the process.



Evidence:



\- external cases;

\- documented experiences;

\- comparative observations.



\---



\## Level 4 — Validated Component



A component demonstrates sufficient evidence.



Evidence:



\- repeated applications;

\- independent confirmation;

\- documented limitations.



\---



\# Research Limitations



Current limitations include:



\- methodology developed primarily through founder-led exploration;

\- limited external applications;

\- limited comparative data;

\- evolving terminology.



These limitations are documented as part of responsible research.



\---
